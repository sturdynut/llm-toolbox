I think cities **should** ban private cars from their central districts, with important caveats.

Here's my honest reasoning:

**The core case is strong:** In dense urban centers, a single private car moving one or two people occupies space that could move dozens via transit, or be used for public life. This isn't a minor inefficiency—it's a fundamental mismatch between the tool and the environment. Once you have density high enough that transit is viable, private cars become a poor use of scarce urban space.

**The secondary benefits are real:** Car-free centers create better air quality for the people with least choice about where they live, enable walkable public spaces that actually make cities valuable to be in, and reduce noise and accidents. These aren't fringe benefits.

**This actually works:** Copenhagen, Amsterdam, parts of Paris, and other places prove this isn't theoretical. Life doesn't fall apart—it often improves.

**But here's where I'm firm about the conditions:**

You can't just ban cars and declare victory. The ban only works if you simultaneously have:
- Genuinely good public transit (not aspirational, actually good)
- Real accommodation for disabled people (accessibility permits, good carshare, accessible transit)
- Affordable alternatives (the poor can't just be priced out of the city center)

And you can't just push the problem to ring roads around the city—that's just displacement.

My view: the principle is sound. Most cities implementing it are doing it half-heartedly, and some are doing it wrongly. But the direction is right.
