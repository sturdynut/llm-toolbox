**MOVE 1: Amber plays 5 -> cell 5**
**STATE 1: cell=5 visited=0,5 score Amber=5, Blue=0**

The first move is always clockwise, so 0 + 5 = cell 5. Every tile was legal (1, 2, 3 and 5 all land on unvisited cells). I chose 5 because it scores the most right away.

**MOVE 2: Blue plays 1 -> cell 6**
**STATE 2: cell=6 visited=0,5,6 score Amber=5, Blue=6**

The previous tile was 5. None of Blue's tiles (1, 3, 4, 6) is a multiple of 5, so no sum with 5 can be one either. Every move is clockwise.
- 1 → 6, legal
- 3 → 0, visited
- 4 → 1, legal
- 6 → 3, legal

I chose 1 → 6 because it scores the most.

**MOVE 3: Amber plays 1 -> cell 7**
**STATE 3: cell=7 visited=0,5,6,7 score Amber=12, Blue=6**

The previous tile was 1. The sums are 1+1=2, 2+1=3 and 3+1=4, so every move is clockwise.
- 1 → 7, legal
- 2 → 0, visited
- 3 → 1, legal

I chose 1 → 7 for the highest score.

**MOVE 4: Blue plays 4 -> cell 3**
**STATE 4: cell=3 visited=0,3,5,6,7 score Amber=12, Blue=9**

The previous tile was 1.
- 3: 3+1=4, clockwise, 7 → 2, legal
- 4: 4+1=5, a multiple of 5, so counter-clockwise, 7 → 3, legal
- 6: 6+1=7, clockwise, 7 → 5, visited

I chose 4 → 3 because it scores more than cell 2. It also leaves Amber stuck next turn.

**MOVE 5: Amber plays 2 -> cell 5**
**STATE 5: cell=5 visited=0,3,5,6,7 score Amber=17, Blue=9**

The previous tile was 4.
- 2: 2+4=6, clockwise, 3 → 5, visited
- 3: 3+4=7, clockwise, 3 → 6, visited

Neither tile is legal, so Rule 4 applies. Amber must play her smallest tile, 2, clockwise, and it may land on a visited cell: 3 → 5. Amber scores 5. The set of visited cells doesn't change.

**The rule I'm least sure I applied correctly: Rule 4.**

Turn 5 was the only time I used it, and it has two ambiguities:

1. **When a tile counts as unplayable.** I decided both tiles were illegal by first working out each tile's direction under Rule 2 and then checking where it would land. Another reading is that Rule 4 applies only if a tile fails in both directions. That reading changes nothing here: counter-clockwise, 2 would go 3 → 1 and 3 would go 3 → 0. Cell 1 is unvisited, so tile 2 would have been playable and Rule 4 would not have applied. I think my reading is the intended one, since Rule 2 fixes the direction.
2. **Whether landing on a visited cell still scores.** I gave Amber the full 5 points. The scoring rule says the mover scores the cell the marker ends on and makes no exception for forced moves. Still, you might have intended forced moves to score nothing.

A smaller open question: if the game continued, would the forced tile 2 count as the previous tile for Blue's direction check under Rule 2? I would say yes, but the rules don't say.
