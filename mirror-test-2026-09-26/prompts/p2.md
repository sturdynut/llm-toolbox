Here is a game I just invented, called Ring.

Setup: a ring of 8 cells numbered 0 to 7; cell 7 is next to cell 0. One shared marker starts on cell 0. Two players alternate turns, Amber first. Amber holds tiles {1, 2, 3, 5}. Blue holds tiles {1, 3, 4, 6}. Both scores start at 0.

Rule 1. On your turn you play one tile from your hand and move the marker that many cells. A played tile is gone for good.
Rule 2. Direction: add the tile you are playing to the tile played on the immediately previous turn. If that sum is a multiple of 5, the marker moves counter-clockwise (downward, e.g. 2 → 1 → 0 → 7); otherwise it moves clockwise (upward, e.g. 6 → 7 → 0 → 1). The first move of the game is always clockwise.
Rule 3. A move may not end on a cell the marker has already ended on earlier in the game. Cell 0 counts as already visited at the start. Only the ending cell matters, not the cells passed over.
Rule 4. If none of your tiles can be played without breaking Rule 3, you must play your smallest tile, it moves clockwise regardless of Rule 2, and it may end on a visited cell.
Scoring: after each move, the player who moved adds the number of the cell the marker ends on to their score.

Play 5 turns against yourself (Amber, Blue, Amber, Blue, Amber), choosing whatever moves you like as long as they are legal. For each turn write exactly these two lines, then your reasoning for that move:

MOVE <turn>: <Amber|Blue> plays <tile> -> cell <cell>
STATE <turn>: cell=<cell> visited=<comma-separated cells> score Amber=<n>, Blue=<n>

After turn 5, tell me which rule you are least sure you applied correctly, and why.
