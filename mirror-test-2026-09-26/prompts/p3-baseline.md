Question: Two cards are drawn at random, without replacement, from a standard 52-card deck. Given that at least one of the two cards is red, what is the probability that at least one of them is a heart?

Solution:
1. Let R = "at least one card is red" and H = "at least one card is a heart". We want P(H | R) = P(H and R) / P(R).
2. Every heart is red, so H implies R, which means P(H and R) = P(H).
3. P(no hearts) = (39/52) × (38/51) = 1482/2652.
4. So P(H) = 1 − 1482/2652 = 1170/2652.
5. P(no red cards) = P(both black) = (26/52) × (25/51) = 650/2652.
6. So P(R) = 1 − 650/2652 = 2002/2652.
7. P(H | R) = P(H) / P(R) = (1170/2652) / (2002/2652) = 1170/2002 = 45/77 ≈ 0.584.

Answer: 45/77 ≈ 0.584
